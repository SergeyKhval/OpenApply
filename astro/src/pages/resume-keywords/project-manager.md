We read three project manager postings live in September 2026: an enterprise SaaS implementation role managing a 40+ project portfolio, a capital infrastructure project role at a transportation agency, and a customer-facing technical project role at a public-safety software company. Different industries, but the same underlying ask: a certification signal, proof you've run multiple stakeholders without direct authority over them, and a real budget or portfolio number.

## Must-have keywords

| Keyword | What we saw | How to prove it on your resume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PMP certification (or actively pursuing it) | Required in 2 of 3, preferred in the third | State it plainly near the top; if you don't have it, say what you have instead (CAPM, years in lieu of cert). |
| 5+ years managing projects, with a specific portfolio or budget size | Required in all 3 | Give a real number: budget size, number of concurrent projects, or team size. |
| Stakeholder management in a matrix environment | Required in all 3 | Name the stakeholders (client, engineering, ops, executives) and a conflict you resolved between them. |
| Project planning, scheduling, budgeting, and risk management methodologies | Required in all 3 | Name the actual methodology you used and one risk you caught before it became a problem. |
| Microsoft Office Suite proficiency | Explicit in 2 of 3 | List it only if you also list something more specific; on its own it reads as filler in 2026. |
| Written, verbal, and interpersonal communication | Required in all 3 | Point to a status report or exec update you owned, not just "communication skills." |

## Nice-to-have keywords

| Keyword | What we saw | How to prove it on your resume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agile, Waterfall, or hybrid delivery methodology knowledge | Preferred, industry-dependent | Name the methodology and a project where you actually used it, not a certification alone. |
| Industry-specific certifications (CCM, PE, RA for construction/infrastructure) | Preferred at the infrastructure-focused company | Only list a certification you currently hold and can produce. |
| Project or portfolio tools (Monday.com, Confluence, or similar) | Mentioned as tools used, not gating | Name the tool and what you tracked in it. |
| Domain-specific technical knowledge (networking, security, or construction systems) | Preferred where the projects were technical | State the technical area and your depth in it honestly. |
| GovCon, AEC, or public-sector project experience | A plus at the companies that needed it | Name the sector and a constraint specific to it (procurement rules, compliance). |

## How to use this without keyword-stuffing

Certifications and tool names are table stakes; what separates candidates is a real number attached to a real outcome. "Managed cross-functional projects" says nothing. "Managed a $2M portfolio of 12 concurrent implementations, cutting average time-to-go-live by 3 weeks" says everything a reviewer needs.

We read five software engineer postings that were live in September 2026, at an AI infrastructure company, a database platform, a consumer fintech app, an edge-computing systems company, and a fintech B2B startup, ranging from mid-level to senior. Below is what actually showed up as required versus preferred, not a generic list of "skills recruiters love." If a keyword isn't true of you, don't add it. The point of this list is to help you find the real, honest overlap between your experience and what's being asked for, so you know what to lead with and what gap to address before you apply.

## Must-have keywords

These showed up as explicit requirements in most of the postings we reviewed.

| Keyword | How often it was required | How to prove it on your resume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A primary language, in depth (Go, Python, TypeScript, Java, C++, or Rust) | 5 of 5 | Name the language next to a project where you owned real design decisions in it, not just "used Python." |
| 4+ years of professional software engineering experience | 5 of 5 | State your actual years and the kind of systems you built (production, customer-facing, internal tooling). |
| Distributed systems or scalable systems design | 4 of 5 | Describe a system you built or maintained that had to handle scale, failure, or concurrency, and what broke when it didn't. |
| Cloud-native infrastructure (AWS, GCP, Azure, Kubernetes, containers) | 4 of 5 | List the specific services you've operated, not just "AWS," and what you used them for. |
| Clear technical communication | 3 of 5 | Point to a design doc, RFC, or a time you explained a tradeoff that changed a decision. |
| Hands-on use of AI coding tools (Copilot, Cursor, Claude Code, or similar) in daily work | 3 of 5 | Say which tool and for what: code review, scaffolding, debugging. Vague "AI-assisted" claims read as filler. |

## Nice-to-have keywords

These appeared in some postings, usually as a differentiator rather than a gate.

| Keyword | How often it appeared | How to prove it on your resume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LLM or AI agent systems in production (RAG, tool-calling, orchestration frameworks) | 2 of 5 | Name the specific pattern you shipped, not "worked with AI." |
| Open-source contributions | 2 of 5 | Link the repo and say what you actually merged. |
| A second language (Ruby, Rust, Zig, or similar) | 2 of 5 | Only list it if you could debug a production issue in it today. |
| Mentoring or technical leadership | 2 of 5 | Name how many engineers, and one concrete thing you unblocked or improved for them. |
| CI/CD pipeline ownership (GitHub Actions or equivalent) | 2 of 5 | Describe what the pipeline did and a metric it improved (deploy time, flakiness, incident rate). |

## How to use this without keyword-stuffing

ATS systems and human reviewers are both looking for the same thing: evidence, not vocabulary. Work each must-have keyword into a bullet that already exists in your experience section, with a real outcome attached. If you're missing 2 or more of the must-haves for a specific posting, that's useful information: either the role is a stretch, or you have the experience but haven't written it down yet.

The keyword list above is a starting point built from a handful of real postings. It will drift out of date, and it can't see the specific posting you're applying to. Once you have that posting in hand, paste it in below alongside your resume for a line-by-line check against that exact job, not an average of five others.
